One short screen. Three piles.

No treemaps, no list of ten thousand files. Hoardini sorts the big stuff into three groups, so you only think about the things that need you.

  1. Junk you won't miss

    One press

    Temporary files, app and browser caches, Windows leftovers and the Recycle Bin. Programs make these again when they need them. One press clears the lot.

  2. Free space, keep the files

    Nothing lost

    Folders that can be compressed in place, and cloud files that can stay online only. Every file is still there when you open it. They just take less room on this PC.

  3. Your call

    You decide

    The big things only you can judge, biggest first. Open one and Hoardini says what it is and why he thinks so. Then delete it, shrink it, move it to another drive, or keep it.

Ask “what is this?” Get a straight answer.

Open anything on the list and Hoardini tells you what it is, in plain words, and shows you how he knows.

He checks the evidence first: a catalog of known places on Windows, the types of files inside, the list of installed programs, and whether any program has the files open. Then the AI explains what he found. He searches the web only when that isn't enough.

Not sure? Ask him about it, right there.

Example

Nothing happens until you press.

Hoardini is careful with your files, and he never has the last word. You do.

The answer is the action.
Every explanation ends in a button that does it. Press it and it happens. Don't, and nothing changes.
Delete means delete.
You hold the button to delete, and the files skip the Recycle Bin. The bin frees nothing until you empty it, so Hoardini doesn't pretend.
Your word wins.
His guesses never outrank you. What you say to keep stays kept, and built-in rules hold back the places Windows needs.
He remembers quietly.
Things you keep stop showing up. One link brings them back if you change your mind.
He stays out of the way.
One window. He keeps track of your drive in the background, at low priority, so your PC stays fast.

Choose a brain.

Hoardini needs an AI model to explain what he finds. The first-run screen sets one up in a few clicks.

Online choices send your questions, and the file and folder names Hoardini looks up, to that provider. The PC option sends nothing anywhere.

  • On this PC Recommended

    Granite 4.1 3B downloads once and runs on your processor. Nothing leaves your PC, and it works offline. Qwen3.5 9B is smarter if you have a graphics card. LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp, vLLM or any .gguf file work too.

  • A free online brain

    Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud, Mistral, Cloudflare Workers AI or Z.ai. The setup screen says how much each one allows, whether it needs a card, and whether it may learn from your questions.

  • Your own provider

    OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, Together and Cerebras are ready to pick. Any OpenAI-compatible service works with its address and a key.
