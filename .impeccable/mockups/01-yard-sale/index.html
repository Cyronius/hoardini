“What is this?”

Open anything on the table and he says what it is and why he thinks so. He checks the facts first and guesses last:

  1. Where it lives. A catalog of places Windows and common apps keep things.
  2. What's inside. The kinds of files, and how old they are.
  3. Who owns it. The list of programs installed on your PC.
  4. Whether it's in use. A check for files a program has open right now.
  5. Then the AI explains, in plain words.
  6. The web comes last, only if he still isn't sure.

House rules

  • Nothing goes until you press. He suggests. You decide.
  • Delete means hold the button. No slips from one stray click.
  • Delete is for real. It skips the Recycle Bin, because the bin frees nothing until it's emptied.
  • Your word beats his guess. What you said to keep is held back, every time.
  • Keep it once and it stops showing up. One link brings it back.
  • One window, then he gets out of the way. No tray icon, no pop-ups. The list of your drive stays fresh quietly, in the background.

Recommended. Nothing leaves your PC, and it works offline.

Granite 4.1 3B downloads once and runs on your processor. Qwen3.5 9B is smarter but needs a graphics card. Any .gguf file you already have works too, and so do LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp and vLLM.

A free online brain

Built-in setup for Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud, Mistral, Cloudflare Workers AI and Z.ai. The setup screen says how much each allows, whether a card is needed, and whether it may learn from your questions.

Your own provider

OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, Together and Cerebras are ready to go. Any OpenAI-compatible service works with an address and a key.

Online choices send your questions, and the file and folder names he looks up, to that provider. On this PC, nothing is sent anywhere.
